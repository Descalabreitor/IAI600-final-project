\begin{abstract}
We study how to forecast the 24 hourly price changes of Bitcoin's next
day from the preceding hourly data. On 59{,}331 consecutive hourly
Bitstamp candles (January 2020 -- October 2026), we build scale-free
features, split the data into chronological train, validation and test
blocks with a purge between them, make every choice without the test
block, and compare regularised linear models, nearest neighbours, support
vector machines, tree ensembles, neural networks (MLP, LSTM) and a
pretrained time-series foundation model (Timestral, zero-shot) against
simple rules, with block-bootstrap confidence intervals. No model
forecasts the hourly returns better than ``no change'': at the hourly
scale Bitcoin behaves like a random walk, and only the next hour carries a
small reversal signal (AUC 0.538). The \emph{size} of the moves, however,
is clearly predictable. Gradient Boosting refitted monthly explains
14.8\% of the variance of each hourly absolute return (2.6 times a
volatility rule), an LSTM fine-tuned monthly 15.6\%, zero-shot Timestral
15.7\%, and the average of Timestral and Gradient Boosting 16.4\%.
Conformally calibrated 80\% ranges cover 80\% of the test hours, and
next-day volatility is predicted with $R^2 \approx 0.51$. Keeping models
up to date mattered more than any extra feature or data source.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The assigned task is to predict, for each of the 24 hours of the next
day, how much the price of Bitcoin will change, $\ret_h =
\mathrm{Close}_h/\mathrm{Close}_{h-1}-1$, from the hours before. Such
short-horizon forecasts matter for risk management, position sizing and
the timing of trades. Bitcoin is a demanding case: it trades 24/7, its
returns are heavy-tailed, and its behaviour shifts between bull and bear
markets and after events such as the launch of the US spot ETFs in
January 2024.

Two classical results frame the problem. In an efficient market, past
prices should not predict the direction of future
returns~\cite{fama1970}; the size of the moves, on the other hand,
clusters in time and is predictable~\cite{engle1982,bollerslev1986}.
Financial time series also make it easy to fool oneself: overlapping
windows and random splits leak the future into the training
data~\cite{lopezdeprado2018}. We therefore let a data analysis decide
which questions are answerable, evaluate every model against a simple
rule on a test period never used for choices, and finally ask what the
forecasts are worth to a trader.
